SECTION C: SHORT ANSWER TYPE-I

5 Questions × 4 Marks = 20 Marks

Q21. Orientation Polarization
Explain the mechanism of orientation polarization in polar dielectrics. Discuss the effect of temperature on the alignment of permanent electric dipoles in the presence of an external electric field.
Q22. Binomial Distribution — Numerical
A fair coin is tossed 8 times. Calculate the probability of obtaining exactly 5 heads. Also determine the mean and variance of the corresponding binomial distribution.
Q23. Poisson Distribution — Numerical
The average number of defects in a manufactured component is 2.5. Assuming that defects follow a Poisson distribution, calculate the probability that a randomly selected component contains exactly 3 defects.
Q24. Normal Distribution — Numerical
The marks obtained by students in an examination are normally distributed with a mean of 60 and a standard deviation of 5. Calculate the probability that a randomly selected student scores between 55 and 65 marks. Use the standard normal distribution table.
Q25. Normal Distribution — Numerical
The lifetimes of a particular type of electronic component follow a normal distribution with a mean of 1,000 hours and a standard deviation of 100 hours. Determine the probability that a component operates for more than 1,200 hours.

SECTION D: SHORT ANSWER TYPE-II

5 Questions × 6 Marks = 30 Marks

Q26. Gauss's Law in a Dielectric Medium
State Gauss's law for the electric displacement vector. Derive the integral form of the law for a dielectric medium containing free charges, and explain the physical distinction between free charge and bound charge.
Q27. Binomial Distribution — Numerical
A manufacturing process produces defective items with a probability of 0.1. A random sample of 10 items is selected independently.
(a) Calculate the probability that exactly two items are defective.
(b) Calculate the probability that at least one item is defective.
(c) Determine the expected number of defective items.
Q28. Poisson Distribution — Numerical
A call centre receives an average of 4 calls per minute. Assuming that the number of calls follows a Poisson distribution, determine:
(a) The probability of receiving exactly 2 calls in a minute.
(b) The probability of receiving no calls in a minute.
(c) The probability of receiving more than 2 calls in a minute.
Q29. Normal Distribution — Numerical
The scores of 1,000 candidates in an examination are normally distributed with a mean of 39.5 marks and a standard deviation of 12.5 marks. A minimum score of 60 marks is required to obtain first-class status. Using the standard normal distribution table, estimate the number of candidates who obtain first class. Show the standardization procedure and calculation of the required tail probability.
Q30. Dielectric Polarization
Define dielectric polarization and explain its principal mechanisms. Discuss electronic, ionic and orientation polarization, distinguishing the physical processes responsible for each mechanism.

SECTION E: LONG ANSWER QUESTIONS

2 Questions × 10 Marks = 20 Marks

Q31. Dielectrics: Classification and Polarization (10 Marks)

(a) Define a dielectric material and explain its behaviour when subjected to an external electric field. (2 Marks)

(b) Distinguish between polar and non-polar dielectric molecules on the basis of their permanent electric dipole moments. Give suitable examples. (2 Marks)

(c) For a non-polar dielectric consisting of N molecules per unit volume, derive the expression for electronic polarization in terms of molecular polarizability and the applied electric field. State the assumptions involved. (3 Marks)

(d) For a polar dielectric under a weak external electric field, derive the expression for orientation polarization under thermal equilibrium. Explain the role of thermal agitation and state the limitations of the approximation used. (3 Marks)

Q32. Normal Distribution — Comprehensive Numerical (10 Marks)
In a university examination, the marks obtained by 5,000 candidates are normally distributed with a mean of 39.5 marks and a standard deviation of 12.5 marks. The maximum possible score is 100 marks. A candidate must obtain at least 60 marks to secure first-class status.
(a) Define the normal probability density function and explain the significance of its mean and standard deviation. (2 Marks)
(b) Calculate the standardized score corresponding to 60 marks. (2 Marks)
(c) Using the standard normal distribution table, determine the probability that a candidate scores at least 60 marks. (3 Marks)
(d) Estimate the number of candidates who secure first class. (3 Marks)
