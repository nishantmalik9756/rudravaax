5. What is Polarization?
Polarization is the displacement or alignment of bound positive and negative charges in a dielectric when an external electric field is applied. It produces electric dipoles or aligns existing dipoles.
6. What is Spontaneous Emission?
Spontaneous emission occurs when an excited atom or molecule returns to a lower energy state on its own and emits a photon without any external photon triggering the transition.
7. What is Stimulated Emission?
Stimulated emission occurs when an incident photon causes an excited atom to drop to a lower energy state, emitting a second photon with the same energy, frequency, phase, direction and polarization.
8. Define the Binomial Distribution.
The binomial distribution describes the number of successes in a fixed number of independent trials, each having the same probability of success p.
9. Define the Poisson Distribution.
The Poisson distribution describes the number of events occurring independently in a fixed interval when the average event rate is constant.
10. Define the Normal Distribution.
The normal distribution is a continuous, symmetric, bell-shaped probability distribution characterized by its mean μ and standard deviation σ.

SECTION C — Short Answers (5 × 4 = 20 Marks)

1. Explain Orientation Polarization.
Orientation polarization occurs in polar dielectrics that possess permanent molecular dipole moments. Without an electric field, the dipoles are randomly oriented, so the net polarization is approximately zero. When an electric field is applied, the dipoles tend to align with the field, producing a net dipole moment per unit volume. Thermal motion opposes alignment; therefore, orientation polarization generally decreases as temperature increases.
For a weak electric field: P = Nμ²E / (3kT) Where: P = Orientation polarization N = Number of molecules per unit volume μ = Permanent dipole moment E = Applied electric field k = Boltzmann constant T = Absolute temperature
2. A fair coin is tossed 8 times. Find the probability of exactly 5 heads, the mean and the variance.
Given: n = 8, p = 0.5, q = 0.5 Let X follow Binomial Distribution.
P(X = 5) = C(8,5)(0.5)⁵(0.5)³ = 56/256 = 0.21875
Mean = np = 8 × 0.5 = 4
Variance = npq = 8 × 0.5 × 0.5 = 2
3. If the mean number of defects is 2.5, find the probability of exactly 3 defects.
Given: λ = 2.5, x = 3
P(X = x) = e⁻λ λˣ / x! P(X = 3) = e⁻²·⁵ (2.5)³ / 3! P(X = 3) ≈ 0.2138
4. A normal distribution has mean 60 and standard deviation 5. Find P(55 < X < 65).
Given: μ = 60, σ = 5 Z = (X − μ) / σ
At X = 55: Z = (55 − 60) / 5 = −1 At X = 65: Z = (65 − 60) / 5 = 1 Therefore: P(55 < X < 65) = P(−1 < Z < 1) = 0.6827 = 68.27%
5. A product lifetime is normally distributed with mean 1000 hours and standard deviation 100 hours. Find the probability that it lasts more than 1200 hours.
Given: μ = 1000, σ = 100 Z = (1200 − 1000) / 100 = 2
P(X > 1200) = P(Z > 2) = 1 − 0.9772 = 0.0228 = 2.28%

SECTION D — Long Short-Answer Questions (5 × 6 = 30 Marks)

1. State Gauss's Law for a Dielectric.
Gauss's law for the electric displacement field D states that the total electric flux through a closed surface equals the free charge enclosed by that surface.
∮ D · dA = Qfree, enclosed Differential form: ∇ · D = ρfree
For a linear dielectric: D = εE Also: D = ε₀E + P Where: D = Electric displacement field E = Electric field ε = Permittivity of the medium ε₀ = Permittivity of free space P = Polarization ρfree = Free charge density For a homogeneous dielectric: ε = ε₀εᵣ Thus, Gauss's law relates the electric flux of D through a closed surface to the free charge enclosed.
2. For a binomial distribution with n = 10 and p = 0.1, find P(X = 2), P(X ≥ 1), and the expected value.
Given: n = 10, p = 0.1, q = 0.9
P(X = 2) = C(10,2)(0.1)²(0.9)⁸ = 45 × 0.01 × 0.430467 ≈ 0.1937
P(X ≥ 1) = 1 − P(X = 0) = 1 − (0.9)¹⁰ ≈ 0.6513
Expected Value = np = 10 × 0.1 = 1
3. Calls arrive according to a Poisson distribution with mean 4 per interval. Find P(X = 2), P(X = 0), and P(X > 2).
Given: λ = 4
P(X = 2) = e⁻⁴ × 4² / 2! ≈ 0.1465
P(X = 0) = e⁻⁴ ≈ 0.0183
P(X > 2) = 1 − [P(0) + P(1) + P(2)] = 1 − e⁻⁴(1 + 4 + 8) ≈ 0.7619
4. In a group of 1000 candidates, marks are normally distributed with mean 39.5 and standard deviation 12.5. Estimate the number scoring at least 60.
Given: N = 1000 μ = 39.5 σ = 12.5 X = 60
Z = (X − μ) / σ = (60 − 39.5) / 12.5 = 1.64
From the standard normal table: P(Z ≥ 1.64) ≈ 0.0505
Number of candidates = 1000 × 0.0505 = 50.5 ≈ 51 candidates
5. Explain the Mechanisms of Dielectric Polarization.
1. Electronic Polarization: It occurs when the electron cloud shifts slightly relative to the nucleus under an applied electric field. It occurs in almost all atoms and molecules.
2. Ionic Polarization: In ionic materials, positive and negative ions shift in opposite directions under an applied electric field.
3. Orientation Polarization: Permanent molecular dipoles tend to align with the applied electric field. Thermal motion opposes alignment.
4. Space-Charge Polarization: Mobile charges accumulate near interfaces, defects and boundaries, producing polarization.
Total Polarization: P = Pₑ + Pᵢ + Pₒ + Psc
The total polarization is the sum of the polarization mechanisms present in the dielectric material.

SECTION E — Very Long Answers (2 × 10 = 20 Marks)

1. Distinguish between polar and non-polar dielectrics. Explain electronic polarization and derive orientation polarization.

Polar Dielectrics

Polar dielectrics have molecules possessing a permanent electric dipole moment even without an external field. Examples: Water (H₂O) and HCl. Without an external field, the dipoles are randomly oriented. When an electric field is applied, the dipoles tend to align with the field.

Non-Polar Dielectrics

Non-polar dielectrics do not have a permanent molecular dipole moment because their positive and negative charge centres coincide or their molecular symmetry cancels the dipole moment. Examples: N₂ and O₂. When an electric field is applied, the charge centres shift slightly and an induced dipole moment is produced.

Electronic Polarization Derivation

Consider an atom with a spherical electron cloud of radius r and total charge −Ze around a nucleus of charge +Ze. When an electric field E is applied, the electron cloud and nucleus are displaced relative to one another by a small distance x. The induced dipole moment is:
p = Ze x
In the simplified spherical electron-cloud model, the restoring electric field is:
Eᵣ = Zex / (4πε₀r³)
At equilibrium, the applied field balances the restoring field:
E = Zex / (4πε₀r³) Therefore: x = 4πε₀r³E / (Ze)
Since p = Ze x:
p = Ze × 4πε₀r³E / (Ze) p = 4πε₀r³E
Electronic polarizability αₑ is defined by p = αₑE. Therefore:
αₑ = 4πε₀r³ Electronic Polarization: Pₑ = NαₑE
Where N is the number of atoms per unit volume.

Orientation Polarization Derivation

Consider a polar molecule with a permanent dipole moment μ placed in an electric field E. If θ is the angle between the dipole and the field, its potential energy is:
U = −μE cos θ
Due to thermal motion, the dipoles are not perfectly aligned with the field. For a weak electric field, the average component of the dipole moment along the field is approximately:
⟨μ cos θ⟩ = μ²E / (3kT)
If N is the number of molecules per unit volume, orientation polarization is:
Pₒ = N⟨μ cos θ⟩ Therefore: Pₒ = Nμ²E / (3kT)
Thus, orientation polarization is directly proportional to the applied electric field and inversely proportional to absolute temperature in the weak-field limit. Electronic polarization is caused by displacement of the electron cloud and is much less dependent on temperature.

2. Marks of 5000 candidates are normally distributed with mean 39.5 and standard deviation 12.5. Estimate how many score 60 or above.
Given: Total candidates N = 5000 Mean μ = 39.5 Standard deviation σ = 12.5 Required score X = 60
Z = (X − μ) / σ Z = (60 − 39.5) / 12.5 Z = 20.5 / 12.5 Z = 1.64
From the standard normal distribution table:
Φ(1.64) ≈ 0.9495
Therefore:
P(X ≥ 60) = P(Z ≥ 1.64) = 1 − 0.9495 = 0.0505
Expected number of candidates:
= 5000 × 0.0505 = 252.5 ≈ 253 candidates
Therefore, approximately 253 candidates are expected to score 60 or above.
